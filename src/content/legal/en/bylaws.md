## 1. Name, domicile and language

The name of the association is Kuutti ry and its domicile is Espoo.

The association’s official language is Finnish. Finnish, Swedish, English or another language may be used in its activities.

## 2. Purpose and activities

The association’s purpose is to promote safe, free and equal opportunities for everyone to meet other people and form relationships in Finland, to reduce loneliness, to support scientific research on relationships and loneliness, and to maintain an open community in which volunteers develop the service.

Most services on the market that help people meet operate under a business model in which the provider’s income depends on the time users spend in the service and bears no relation to the relationships they form. The association was founded to provide such a service free of this conflict of interest. The association measures its success by how many users find what they are looking for and no longer need the service. The source code is public so that anyone can examine how the service works.

To pursue its purpose, the association operates an open-source information society service, works with research and educational institutions, organises events and publishes material.

To support its activities, the association may accept grants, donations and bequests, own the real, movable and intangible property it needs for its activities, and conduct lotteries and money collections after obtaining the appropriate permit.

The association may donate funds to a university, a university of applied sciences, VTT Technical Research Centre of Finland Ltd, the Finnish Institute for Health and Welfare, the Finnish Institute of Occupational Health, the VATT Institute for Economic Research or a comparable research institution governed by public law and domiciled in the European Union.

The funding of the association’s activities does not depend on how much time users spend in the information society service it operates. The association does not sell advertising space or display third-party advertisements in its service. It does not disclose its users’ personal data to another controller for a commercial purpose, or provide any other data arising from use of the service for commercial use. It does not enter into agreements with entities whose principal business is providing matchmaking or dating services to consumers, or with entities in the same corporate group. It accepts no funds, loans or other financing from such entities. The restrictions in this section and in sections 11 and 13 ensure that the funds entrusted to the association, the volunteer work contributed to it and the service it creates continue to serve this purpose and do not pass into commercial use.

The association operates on a non-profit basis. It does not pay dividends or otherwise distribute a financial surplus to its members.

## 3. Members

An individual who accepts the association’s purpose may be admitted as an ordinary member. An individual or a legally competent organisation that wishes to support the association’s purpose and activities may be admitted as a supporting member. The board admits ordinary and supporting members on application. On the board’s proposal, a meeting of the association may invite a person who has significantly advanced and supported the association’s activities to become honorary chair or an honorary member, provided the person has consented to membership.

A person’s membership category or lack of membership has no bearing on their position or benefits in the information society service that the association operates.

## 4. Admission and membership fees

The annual meeting sets the admission fee and annual membership fee separately for ordinary members and supporting members. The honorary chair and honorary members pay no membership fees.

## 5. Board

The board manages the association’s affairs. The annual meeting elects its chair, two to four other ordinary board members, and up to two deputy members. Board members may be chosen from within or outside the association’s membership. The board serves from one annual meeting to the next. It elects a vice-chair from among its members and appoints a secretary, a treasurer and any other officers it needs from among its members or from outside the board. The chair, or the vice-chair when the chair is unavailable, convenes the board when they consider it necessary or when at least half of the board members request it.

The board has a quorum when at least half its members are present, including the chair or vice-chair. The board may take a decision without a meeting if every board member has an opportunity to participate in the decision.

Votes are decided by an absolute majority. In a tie, the chair has the casting vote, except in elections, where a tie is decided by lot.

## 6. Signing for the association

Any two of the board chair, vice-chair and other board members may sign jointly on behalf of the association.

## 7. Financial year

The association’s financial year is the calendar year.

## 8. Meetings of the association

Members may also participate in a meeting by post or by using a telecommunications connection and other technical means, during or before the meeting. The board may decide to hold a meeting entirely by such electronic means without a physical venue.

A member may appoint a representative by proxy to attend a meeting and exercise their voting rights. A person may represent no more than one member at a meeting. A representative who is also a member may vote both by proxy and on their own behalf.

The annual meeting is held each year between January and May, on a date set by the board.

An extraordinary meeting is held when a meeting of the association so decides, when the board considers it necessary, or when at least one tenth (1/10) of the members entitled to vote request a meeting from the board in writing for a specifically stated matter. The board must convene the meeting without delay upon receiving the request.

Each ordinary member, the honorary chair and each honorary member has one vote at meetings once they have been a member for at least twelve (12) months. Founding members have voting rights immediately. Supporting members and members whose voting rights have yet to take effect because of the waiting period have the right to attend and speak at meetings.

Unless these bylaws provide otherwise, a meeting adopts the position supported by more than half of the votes cast. In a tie, the meeting chair has the casting vote, except in elections, where a tie is decided by lot.

## 9. Notice of meetings

The board must give members at least thirty (30) days’ notice of meetings of the association by letter sent by post or by email.

## 10. Annual meeting

The annual meeting deals with the following matters.

1. Open the meeting.
2. Elect the meeting chair, a secretary, two persons to scrutinise the minutes and, if needed, two vote counters.
3. Establish that the meeting has been duly convened and has a quorum.
4. Approve the agenda.
5. Present the financial statements or annual accounts, the annual report and the report of the operations examiners or auditors.
6. Decide whether to approve the financial statements or annual accounts and discharge the board and other accountable persons from liability.
7. Adopt the action plan and budget and set the admission and membership fees.
8. Elect the board chair and other board members.
9. Elect one or two operations examiners and deputy operations examiners, or one or two auditors and deputy auditors.
10. Deal with other matters specified in the notice of the meeting.

A member who wishes to have a matter considered at the annual meeting must notify the board in writing early enough for it to be included in the notice.

## 11. Intangible rights

The association acquires and retains ownership of the intangible rights it needs for its activities, including trademarks and domain names.

The association may transfer ownership of its trademarks or domain names only upon dissolution, and may not pledge them. It may transfer the service it operates, its user accounts, app store listings or the infrastructure used to provide the service to another party only upon dissolution. It does not license or grant rights to use its trademarks or domain names for commercial purposes. Any right of use granted excludes the right to sublicense.

The association keeps the software code used to implement its information society service publicly available under an open-source copyleft licence. The licence must require anyone who changes or adapts the source code to make the modified source code publicly available under the same or equivalent licence terms, including when the modified version is used to provide a service to the public over a network (for example, the GNU Affero General Public License or an equivalent licence). This requirement does not apply to third-party software components that were not developed for the association’s service and are used under their own licence terms.

## 12. Resignation, expulsion and change of membership category

A member may resign by notifying the board or its chair in writing or by giving notice of resignation at a meeting of the association for entry in the minutes. An ordinary member may give notice in the same way that they are becoming a supporting member.

In addition to the grounds set out in section 14 of the Associations Act, a member may be expelled if, in the name of or on behalf of the association, they have taken or sought to take an action prohibited by section 2 or 11 of these bylaws. Voting or expressing an opinion at a meeting of the association does not constitute grounds for expulsion.

A meeting of the association may decide to expel an ordinary member, honorary member or honorary chair either unanimously or with at least four votes in favour and no more than one against. The provisions of the Associations Act apply to establishing the reason for expulsion and giving the member an opportunity to respond. The notice of the meeting must mention the proposed expulsion and the meeting must be convened with at least thirty (30) days’ notice.

The board decides on the expulsion of a supporting member.

The board may expel a member for failure to pay a membership fee when it is at least six (6) months overdue. The board may make this decision by a simple majority of its members. Before taking the decision, it must send the member a written demand for payment and allow at least thirty (30) days for payment.

## 13. Amendment of the bylaws and dissolution of the association

A decision to amend the bylaws, other than the special provisions below, requires a majority of at least three quarters (3/4) of the votes cast at a meeting of the association. The notice of the meeting must mention the proposed amendment and include its wording. If no more than three (3) votes are represented at a meeting considering an amendment, the amendment requires the specified qualified majority at two (2) consecutive meetings.

A decision to dissolve the association or amend section 2, 3, 8, 11, 12 or 13 (the “special provisions”) must be adopted in identical terms and unanimously at two (2) consecutive meetings of the association. Adding a provision that conflicts with a special provision also constitutes an amendment of that special provision. The notice of the meeting must mention the proposed dissolution or amendment of the special provisions and include the wording of the proposed change. If no more than three (3) votes are represented at a meeting considering dissolution or amendment of the special provisions, three (3) consecutive meetings must unanimously support the proposal.

Meetings considering an amendment to the bylaws or dissolution of the association must take place at least thirty (30) days apart.

Upon dissolution, the association’s assets shall be used to advance its purpose in the manner determined by the meeting that decides on dissolution. If the association is terminated by order, its assets shall be used for the same purpose. The assets shall be transferred in the first instance to another Finnish non-profit association and in the second instance to a university or another research or educational institution. The transfer is conditional on the recipient undertaking in writing to comply with the restrictions in sections 2 and 11 of these bylaws in respect of the property and rights transferred, and to transfer them onwards only on the same terms.

The provisions above concerning the association’s assets upon dissolution apply to intangible rights as well as funds. Personal data concerning users of the information society service operated by the association shall, however, be destroyed upon dissolution or termination of the association and shall not be transferred.
